AI Cleaning Instruction:
"Standardize all energy values to MWh, unify IGACOS/Samal identifiers, aggregate barangay-level entries into city-level annual indicators, impute missing values using median-based interpolation per year cluster, and remove outliers exceeding 3 standard deviations. Output a cleaned time-series dataset from 2019–2024."
Result: Successfully normalized multi-source LGU submissions into a unified annual dataset covering energy demand, tourism recovery index, and waste generation trends for IGACOS (2019–2024).

3. Human Analytical Narrative (The "Why" Factor)

The dataset reveals a strong post-pandemic structural recovery pattern in IGACOS, with synchronized growth across energy consumption, tourism activity, and waste generation.

The AI-generated summary initially attributed these trends solely to general economic rebound; however, deeper contextual interpretation shows a more localized mechanism:

The 2020 contraction across all indicators aligns with severe mobility restrictions affecting ferry transport and tourism access to Samal Island.
The gradual rise from 2021–2024 reflects reactivation of tourism flows, increased local commerce, and expanding residential energy demand.
The parallel movement of waste generation and tourism recovery indicates a direct environmental pressure loop tied to visitor inflows rather than population growth alone.

This suggests IGACOS is transitioning from a seasonal tourism-dependent island economy into a high-pressure hybrid coastal development zone, where infrastructure demand is accelerating faster than environmental management systems.

From a policy standpoint, this highlights the need for:

Scaled island waste management systems
Distributed energy expansion (including renewable microgrids)
Tourism carrying-capacity monitoring mechanisms
